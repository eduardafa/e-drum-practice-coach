Privacy

Last updated November 12, 2026

In short:

  • You can practice without an account. Then nothing about you leaves your browser.
  • If you sign in, the app keeps which service you used (GitHub or Google), that service's account number for you, and your name and profile picture from it, to show who's signed in. Never your email.
  • No analytics, no ads, no tracking cookies. Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared except a practice summary sent to Anthropic if you ask for AI coaching tips.

Kept in your browser

Without an account, all of this stays on your device and is never sent anywhere:

  • Settings: tempo, metronome, skill level, latency calibration, the exercise on the stand and similar choices.
  • Practice history: each finished session and tempo-trainer run: the exercise, tempo, when and for how long you played, your time zone, and how each note went (its timing and how hard you hit).
  • Your kit's signals: the notes your drum kit sends over MIDI are scored in the browser as you play. They are never recorded or sent, even when you're signed in.

You can delete your history with Clear history in the History panel, or delete everything by clearing this site's data in your browser.

If you sign in

Signing in lets your practice history follow you to your other devices. You sign in on GitHub's or Google's own page; the app never sees your password. In return, the app receives and stores:

WhatWhyKept
Which service you used, and its account number for you (for example, GitHub user 12345678) To recognize you next time Until you ask for your account to be deleted
Your name and a link to your profile picture, as GitHub or Google show them To show who's signed in, in the account menu Updated each time you sign in; deleted with your account
A session for each signed-in browser (a scrambled form of the sign-in cookie, and when it expires) To keep you signed in Up to 30 days, renewed while you keep using the app, or until you sign out
Your practice history, as described above, synced from each browser where you're signed in To show it on your other devices Until you delete it or your account

The app asks GitHub for no permissions beyond your public profile, reads your account number, name and picture from it, and discards the access it was given. From Google it asks only for basic sign-in and your public profile (openid and profile): your account number, name and picture. Never your email, and no access to anything else in your account.

Coaching tips written by AI

Some accounts (currently only the developer's) can ask for coaching tips on the History page. Only when you press the button, the app sends a summary of your last 30 days of practice to Anthropic, whose model Claude writes the tips. The summary holds numbers only: sessions, accuracy and tempo per exercise, timing per hand, foot and pad, and how hard you hit accents and ghost notes. It has no name, picture, account details, exact dates or individual hits. The tips are stored with your account (so they show again later) and deleted with it.

Cookies

Only two, both needed for signing in, and neither used for tracking:

  • __Host-oauth: set while you sign in, to check that the answer from GitHub or Google belongs to the sign-in you started. It lasts 10 minutes.
  • __Host-session: keeps you signed in. It lasts 30 days and is renewed when you use the app.

Scripts on the page can't read them, and other websites can't use them to act as you.

Where it lives

The app runs on Cloudflare. Signed-in data is stored in a Cloudflare D1 database in Eastern North America. Like any website host, Cloudflare handles your IP address and browser details to deliver the site and protect it from attacks, under its own privacy policy. The app itself doesn't log or keep them.

Your profile picture loads straight from GitHub's or Google's image servers, so they see your browser fetch it, as they do whenever you see your picture elsewhere. No referrer is sent with it.

When you sign in, GitHub's or Google's privacy policy applies to what happens on their pages.

Deleting your data

All of these are in the account menu (the button at the top right) or on the History page:

  • Sign out ends that browser's session. Your synced history stays in your account.
  • Sign out and remove your history from this browser also deletes, from that browser only, the sessions synced with your account. Use it on a computer that isn't yours.
  • Clear history on the History page deletes the history saved in that browser. Tick "Also delete it from your account" to delete the synced copy too, so it's gone from every device the next time it syncs; other browsers keep anything they saved themselves until you clear them as well.
  • Delete my account deletes your account, your sign-in details, every session and all synced history from the server, straight away. History saved in a browser stays there until you clear it.
  • You can also remove the app's access from your GitHub settings or Google account.

Limits

To keep the service fair and free to run, each account can sync about 25 MB of history (several thousand sessions), and the number of requests per minute is limited. Past the size limit, new sessions stay in your browser and the account menu says so.

Changes

If this changes, the date at the top changes too. The page's full history is public in the project's code.
